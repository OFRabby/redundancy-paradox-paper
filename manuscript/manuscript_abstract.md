# Abstract

**Word count: 219**

Leave-one-out (LOO) contribution is widely used for ensemble pruning in anomaly detection, yet its reliability as a diagnostic tool has never been validated against known ground truth. We present a two-phase evaluation. First, a controlled synthetic benchmark with 2,000 ensemble configurations where detector roles (harmful, redundant, beneficial) are known, revealing that LOO detects harmful detectors with perfect recall (1.000) but misclassifies 67% of redundant detectors as beneficial (recall 0.327), a pattern we term the *redundancy paradox*. Second, a multi-dataset evaluation across 3 network intrusion detection datasets, 3 categorical encoders, and 20 seeds (180 configurations) demonstrating that sign-conflict between LOO's validation signal and held-out test effect occurs at 48%, independent of ensemble correlation structure (Spearman rho approximately 0). We propose epsilon-VRG, a guardrail that reduces redundancy false-pruning by 66% without retaining harmful detectors. We further show that LOO's directional claims are seed-fragile: Phase 1's encoder-dependent equal-weight superiority over naive-LOO on NSL-KDD does not replicate across 20 seeds (observed in only 4 of 20). Our findings do not advocate abandoning LOO but demonstrate the need for complementary guardrails in NIDS ensemble construction. All code and data are publicly available.
